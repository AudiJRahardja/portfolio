Transkrip Sidang

Real-time transcription of Indonesian court hearings, using speech recognition trained from scratch, speaker labels and a draft of the hearing record.

Nov 2026 – now

The problem

Many Indonesian court hearing records are still typed by hand while people speak. We want a tool that writes a live transcript with speaker roles and a checkable summary, as a draft for the court clerk. The course rule: no external APIs and no pretrained models, so every model is trained from random weights.

My part

Overall pipeline, model training so far, and results analysis.

How it works

7 stages. Pick a stage to see what goes in, what happens and what comes out. Arrow keys move between stages.

Stage 01 of 07

Prepare speech data

Done
Input

Public Indonesian speech: Common Voice 17, FLEURS, LibriVox-Indonesia and YouTube clips from YODAS

Operation

Convert to 16 kHz mono WAV, write the text the way the model must (lower case, numbers as words) and keep test sentences out of training

Output

211 hours of training audio with manifests

  • YODAS captions are uploaded by video owners, so each clip is kept only if our own round-1 model roughly agrees with the caption (at most 35% character difference).
  • Old-spelling LibriVox books ("djam poekoel doea") are converted to modern spelling.
  • A final check removes any training sentence that appears in a dev or test set.

Flow: sidang.data.download >> sidang.data.prepare >> sidang.data.yodas >> manifests/*.jsonl

Stage 02 of 07

Train acoustic model

Done
Input

Log-mel features with SpecAugment, speed changes and noise

Operation

Train our own Conformer-CTC from random weights: 12 blocks, 6.39M parameters, letters as output

Output

The round-2 model the app uses: 22.0% word error rate on Common Voice 17 test with plain greedy decoding

  • Round 1: 37.5 h of read speech, 100 epochs, about 3.6 GPU hours on an RTX 2060.
  • Round 2 added 173.4 h of YouTube speech: Common Voice 24.5% → 22.0%, FLEURS 27.7% → 24.3%.
  • A bigger 26.2M model did worse on every test set (27.2% on Common Voice) within the free Kaggle budget, so the app keeps the small one.

Flow: train/asr.py --config asr_small.toml >> checkpoints/asr/best.pt

Stage 03 of 07

Decode with LM

Done
Input

Letter probabilities from the acoustic model

Operation

Search the best word sequence with our own CTC prefix beam search, scored by our own legal 3-gram language model plus a bonus for legal terms

Output

Better word choice and spelling: Common Voice word errors 22.0% → 14.7%

  • The language model is trained on 203M tokens: Indonesian Wikipedia, 1,202 Constitutional Court (MK) hearing transcripts and our speech training text.
  • A first version made things worse (dev 26.9%) by joining two words into one unknown word; unknown words now get a fixed penalty, checked by a test.
  • Weights were tuned on the dev sets only; the MK test hearings were never used for tuning.

Flow: CTCEngine.log_probs >> BeamDecoder.decode (beam 16, LM, Hotwords) >> words

Stage 04 of 07

Stream live text

Done
Input

Audio from the browser in 0.5 s chunks

Operation

Detect speech, re-transcribe the growing buffer every second and commit only the words two passes agree on

Output

Live transcript: committed words in black, tentative words in grey

  • An utterance ends after 0.8 s of silence, which is when text is finalised and the speaker is decided.
  • Real-time factor 0.044 with beam search and the language model: 30 s of hearing audio in 1.3 s on an RTX 2060.
  • Committed-text latency is not measured yet; the target is about 2 to 3 s behind speech.

Flow: EnergyVAD >> UtteranceSegmenter >> StreamingTranscriber.process >> HypothesisBuffer

Stage 05 of 07

Speakers & roles

Ongoing
Input

Each finished utterance: its audio and its text

Operation

Match the voice to a known speaker or start a new one, then suggest a court role from typical phrases

Output

Speaker labels with suggested roles (Hakim, Penuntut Umum, Saksi...), which the clerk confirms

  • Works now with a spectral baseline embedder and phrase rules ("demi Allah saya bersumpah" suggests a witness).
  • Planned: our own TDNN speaker-embedding model and a role classifier trained on MK transcript turns.
  • The clerk's choice always wins over the suggestion.

Flow: SpectralEmbedder >> OnlineSpeakerClustering.assign >> RoleSuggester.suggest_all

Stage 06 of 07

Tidy & summarise

Done
Input

Final utterances with speaker roles

Operation

Rewrite legal references in standard form, then build a structured summary and an extractive overview

Output

A summary where every point links to the transcript lines it came from

  • "pasal tiga ratus empat puluh ayat satu" becomes "Pasal 340 ayat (1)"; money becomes "Rp500.000.000".
  • The overview only copies real transcript sentences, so it cannot invent facts. The raw model text is kept too.
  • Planned: our own punctuation model and a trained sentence scorer in place of TextRank.

Flow: normalize_text >> extract_legal_refs >> structured_summary >> textrank >> top_sentences

Stage 07 of 07

App & export

Done
Input

The live session: transcript, speakers, edits and summary

Operation

Serve a local web page, save everything to SQLite, and let the clerk edit and export

Output

A .docx draft of the hearing record (BAS), plus .txt, .srt and .json

  • Everything runs on one machine; no audio or text leaves it.
  • A demo mode plays a short fictional hearing through the whole system without a trained model.
  • Tests run with a fake engine and tiny random-weight models, so no GPU or downloads are needed.

Flow: WS /ws/sessions/{id}/live >> LivePipeline.feed >> SQLite >> to_docx

Results so far

No pretrained models are allowed, so there is no outside model to compare against; each score is compared with plain greedy decoding of our own model.

  • 14.7% word error rate, Common Voice 17 test Baseline: Same model, greedy decoding: 22.0% About 1 word in 7 is wrong on read speech from unseen speakers. Own model + own beam search, language model and legal hotwords; trained from random weights on 211 h Source: docs/RESULTS.md
  • 48.9% word error rate on real court hearings Baseline: Greedy decoding: 54.1% Real hearings are much harder: about half the words are still wrong. 5 Constitutional Court (MK) hearings, 6.2 h; far-field mics and edited reference transcripts Source: docs/RESULTS.md
  • 40 / 161 legal references written exactly right Baseline: Greedy decoding: 22 / 161 (13.7%) Articles, law numbers and case numbers come out right about 1 time in 4, almost double before. Same 5 MK hearings; legal-entity recall 24.8% Source: docs/RESULTS.md
  • 0.044 real-time factor 30 seconds of hearing audio is transcribed in 1.3 seconds. RTX 2060 6 GB, beam 16 + LM + hotwords, one CPU core for the search Source: docs/RESULTS.md

What I learned

  • More data helped more than a bigger model: adding 173 h of YouTube speech lowered word errors on both unseen-speaker test sets, while a 26M-parameter model trained on the same data did worse within the free GPU budget.
  • Very deep models can fail silently: the first 16-block run never learned (CER near 100%, then NaN loss). An extra CTC loss halfway through the network fixed it, and the config now stops early when that happens.
  • Read-speech scores hide how hard hearings are: the language model cut word errors by about a third on Common Voice and FLEURS, but real MK hearings are still near 49%, and letter errors there went up because the decoder writes fewer words on unclear audio.

What's next

  • Train on aligned MK hearing audio (Phase 13), and tune the decoder on a legal dev set instead of read speech.
  • Train our own punctuation and capitalisation model (Phase 14).
  • Train our own speaker-embedding model, and measure speaker error (DER) against the spectral baseline (Phase 15).
  • Role classifier and trained summary sentence scorer (Phase 16), then full evaluation, report and demo (Phase 17).
